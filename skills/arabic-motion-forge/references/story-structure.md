# Story structure — from message to shot list

Motion design is editing rhythm plus meaning. Structure the film before you animate anything.

## The minimum spine

| Part | Share of duration | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–8 % | state the question, tension or object. Viewer decides here. |
| Setup | 8–25 % | give the one fact/feeling needed to follow |
| Development | 25–70 % | 2–4 beats, each with its own visual argument |
| Turn | ~70 % | the comparison, the reveal, the twist, the number that lands |
| Resolution | 70–90 % | the claim in one line, visually calm |
| Exit | last 5–8 % | signature, brand, CTA, or a deliberate silence |

A 15 s ad still has all six parts, at 1.2 s / 2.5 s / 6.7 s / 1.7 s / 0.8 s scale.

## Beat sheet (write this before the shot list)

```
# beat sheet — <project>
1  0.0–1.5   hook      one Arabic word on a black frame, impact sound
2  1.5–4.0   setup     hands at the machine, macro, no text
3  4.0–9.0   dev-1     the ingredient chain: 3 objects arrive on separate layers
4  9.0–14.0  dev-2     the wait: clock motif, tempo slows, hold
5 14.0–17.0  turn      the product revealed bigger than expected
6 17.0–20.0  resolve   one-line claim + logo
```

Each beat gets: purpose, on-screen copy (or none), dominant element, motion intensity (1–5),
sound/music cue, and duration. If a beat has no purpose in one clause, cut it.

## Shot list → decisions

For each shot record: id, time range, beat, camera (locked/drift/push/scroll), layers,
transition-in, transition-out, and the one thing the viewer must notice.

Rules that keep the edit alive:

- **Change something every 2–4 s** — a cut, a build, a reveal, a camera move, an accent. Stillness
  longer than 3 s needs a reason (a hold the viewer asked for, a held breath before impact).
- **Alternate density**: dense build → calm hold → dense build. Constant density is the fastest
  way to look amateur.
- **Cut on motion** when cutting within a beat: the outgoing velocity direction continues into the
  incoming shot (angle delta < 25°) unless the cut argues contrast.
- **Settle before a hard cut**: ≥6 frames of low motion so the eye is not mid-saccade.
- **One idea per shot.** If a shot has two arguments, it is two shots.

## Copy and read time

- Arabic reading comfort: 12–15 characters/second for a headline the viewer must read, plus
  ~0.4 s of settle. Longer than that and the viewer starts reading instead of watching.
- Maximum ~7 words on a vertical frame at a time; one clause per build.
- Never animate a sentence word-by-word and expect it to be read; reveal clauses, then hold.
- Numbers get their own beat and their own emphasis; the unit stays small and stable.
- Approved copy is law: fit the layout to the words, never the reverse. If it does not fit,
  propose an edit to the user — with the read-time math.

## Narrative devices that work in motion

- **accumulation** — the frame fills; meaning grows from quantity.
- **subtraction** — strip elements until one remains; the survivor is the claim.
- **transformation** — one object morphs through states; the last state is the message.
- **scale ladder** — molecule → hand → street → city: context through scale, with a real reason
  for each scale change (not a decorative zoom).
- **comparison split** — two states share the frame; the cut point is the argument.
- **evidence board** — claims pin onto a surface, connections draw between them, one connection
  is wrong on purpose and gets removed.

## Audio as structure

Plan sound with the picture, not after it. Voiceover word onsets (measured from the file, ±1 frame) are the skeleton;
music comes in at beat boundaries; silence before the turn is a tool. Do not put a whoosh on
every cut.

## What "story done" looks like

You can describe the film in three sentences, each corresponding to a beat group, and a stranger
watching it once can repeat the message. If not, the problem is the structure, not the easing.
